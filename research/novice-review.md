**Teach notes before Pointing Pair. That is the biggest risk of losing a novice.**

Review basis: all 11 supplied screenshots, fetched live pages, and matching live source and puzzle data. Browser interaction was unavailable, so tap behavior below is source-traced, not a completed phone session. No files changed. Counts describe checked content, not measured user behavior.

## First impression in 3 sentences

“Stop guessing” speaks directly to me, and the warm design makes this feel welcoming. Full House and the box walkthrough make me believe I can learn this. Then the app jumps from placing one number to reading dozens of tiny notes, and I feel several lessons behind.

## Top 10 problems a novice hits

1. **Pointing Pair: I never learned how to use notes.**  
   All beginners crossing from singles are affected. Naked Single names notes, but never makes me add one, explain why it belongs, or remove one. Pointing Pair suddenly presents a board full of them and asks for every removal. **Fix:** add a guided notes exercise, ending with “removing a possibility does not fill the cell.” **Effort: M.**

2. **Wrong answers often tell me the verdict, not the reason.**  
   Lesson and Practice users get useful feedback for a duplicate number. But “The 4 in R2C7 stays. A Pointing Pair doesn’t remove it” leaves me stuck on the same question. Even an incorrect note removal can be rejected without explaining the missing condition. **Fix:** identify the failed check and highlight its evidence. **Effort: M.**

3. **The notes boards look like a colored spreadsheet.**  
   Phone users learning harder patterns must read roughly 10px notes alongside yellow, purple, pink, blue, green, and red marks. In the Y-Wing screenshot, I cannot infer what pink versus blue means. Dark Swordfish keeps the same density problem. **Fix:** introduce a small legend, enlarge the focused cells, and offer a one-digit view. **Effort: M.**

4. **I must count cells to understand the coach.**  
   All beginners meet “box 5” in Full House and coordinates such as “R3C6” later. Coordinates are explained in the intro, but the board has no row, column, or box labels; box numbering is never explained. **Fix:** temporarily show labels during lessons and outline the cell being discussed. **Effort: M.**

5. **Some later hints give me a false explanation for a correct answer.**  
   In local solve traces, **263 steps across 108 of 200 classic puzzles** claim existing copies of a number exclude every other cell. Those steps actually depend on earlier note removals too. I search for blocking numbers that cannot prove the claim. **Fix:** distinguish direct blocking from earlier deductions and show the relevant remaining notes. **Effort: M.**

6. **The Naked Triple hint teaches the wrong search rule.**  
   **28 of 30 triple positions** contain a move whose cells do not all have identical notes, yet the hint asks for “the same three notes.” I could reject the correct pattern because I followed the instruction. **Fix:** say the three cells use only three different digits *between them*. **Effort: S.**

7. **Asking for help makes me feel less successful.**  
   All lesson learners lose stars for hints; the result explicitly says hints “cost stars.” I need help most while learning something new. **Fix:** distinguish “completed with guidance” from “solved independently,” and reward both without framing help as a slip. **Effort: S.**

8. **The game toolbar assumes I already know its modes.**  
   New Play users get six tools without a first-move prompt. Notes changes number entry; Quick changes tap order; the small keypad counts are unexplained. Fill notes also restores possibilities I previously crossed out through logic. **Fix:** teach the default tap order, state the active mode, and warn that refilling replaces deductions. **Effort: M.**

9. **Practice progress does not tell me what I have mastered.**  
   Practice users see “1 / 30,” but re-entering reshuffles and resets that position counter. The solved total counts repeats, so a full meter does not mean 30 distinct positions completed. **Fix:** retain unique position progress and separate guided from independent solves. **Effort: M.**

10. **Rush removes support just when pressure increases.**  
    New Rush players get a timer, penalties, and no Hint button. Wrong-answer reasons appear briefly as disappearing messages. I may learn to skip quickly rather than understand my mistake. **Fix:** offer an untimed warm-up with persistent feedback before the scored run. **Effort: M.**

**Decision:** prioritize the notes bridge and useful wrong-answer explanations. Without them, a welcoming first lesson leads into a course that expects skills it has not taught.

## Copy fixes

- **Current:** “Like guessing a PIN digit when eight of the ten keys are smudge-free: only one is left.”  
  **Suggested:** “Like nine keys on a ring: rule out eight, and only one remains.”

- **Current:** “For a missing digit, check each empty cell: is that digit already in the cell’s column or box?”  
  **Suggested:** “For a row, check each empty cell’s column and box. For a column, check its row and box.”  
  The existing wording does not cover column searches correctly.

- **Current:** “Columns and boxes do the blocking now”  
  **Suggested:** “Check the crossing lines and boxes for the same digit.”

- **Current:** “Can you find three cells that share the same three notes?”  
  **Suggested:** “Can you find three cells whose notes use only three different digits altogether?”

- **Current:** “Look at the 9s in column 6. Do they all line up?”  
  **Suggested:** “Look at the 9 notes in column 6. Are they all inside one box?”  
  They already line up because they are in one column.

- **Current:** “The 2s already on the board cross out every other empty cell, so 2 has one home left: R5C5.”  
  **Suggested, when earlier deductions are required:** “After the earlier note removals, column 5 has only one possible place for 2: R5C5.”

- **Current:** “Hints and wrong taps cost stars. Replay any time for 3 stars.”  
  **Suggested:** “Lesson complete with guidance. Replay when you’re ready to solve it independently.”

- **Current:** “Few clues. Needs a real technique.”  
  **Suggested:** “Fewer clues. May need note-based techniques.”

The Y-Wing road/petrol analogy should be removed. The actual “if this, then that; either way…” explanation is clearer and preserves the logic.

## What delighted me

- The box lesson’s crosses and green answer make the reasoning visible.
- “One cell instead of one digit” explains the difference between two confusing names.
- Nothing is locked; I can revisit lessons and skip a practice position.
- Hints reveal help gradually instead of immediately taking over.
- All **200 classic puzzles** solved locally; **10,782 generated moves** agreed with their stored solutions. The main weakness is teaching the reasoning, not those checked answers.
